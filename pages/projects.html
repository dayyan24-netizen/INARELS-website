Historicizing History Education

Education systems in several African countries have either reduced or eliminated history curriculum at secondary levels. This comparative inquiry examines the forces responsible for disinvestment in history education in Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, and Uganda — engaging academic experts, teachers, parents, community leaders, and policymakers.

Countries Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, Uganda

Language, Historical Literacies and Civic Engagement

A large-scale, cross-cultural comparative program investigating the intersection of language practices, historical literacies, and civic engagement in multilingual African societies. Re-conceptualizes social development in terms of individual attitudes to civic engagement and explores social identity as a mediating variable.

Scope Multilingual African societies
Discipline Sociolinguistics, Civic Studies

Achievement Gap and Intra-racial Dynamics (AGIRD)

Builds on extensive research on the educational experiences of African American students in the United States — including disaggregated data on immigrant and non-immigrant Black students. Explores immigration trends, socialization patterns, and academic achievement, with initial findings suggesting historical literacies may account for sub-cultural dynamics.

Language, Education, and Development

A decade of fieldwork on the imbrications of language, education, and development in Africa has produced extensive empirical evidence of language's significance to educational and social change processes. INARELS researchers are now developing dissemination plans including publication, conferences, and community outreach.
