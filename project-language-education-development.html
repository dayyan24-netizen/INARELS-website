Project Description

A decade of fieldwork on the imbrications of language, education, and development in Africa has produced extensive empirical evidence of the significance of language to educational and social change processes across the continent.

This project represents the accumulated fieldwork knowledge of INARELS researchers — gathered across Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Botswana, South Africa, and India — on the relationship between language choice, language policy, educational access, and development outcomes. It is the foundational empirical project from which many of the network's more targeted inquiries have grown.

INARELS researchers are now developing comprehensive dissemination plans including peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations, and community outreach programs to bring this evidence base to academic audiences, policymakers, and the communities where the fieldwork was conducted.

The project synthesizes a range of data types — including ethnographic fieldwork, policy document analysis, interview data, and school observation records — to build a multi-dimensional picture of how language operates as a lever for or barrier to educational access, quality, and social development across diverse African contexts.

As the most longitudinal of the INARELS research programs, Language, Education, and Development provides the empirical bedrock on which the network's theoretical contributions to the field of language-in-education research are built. The dissemination phase represents an important moment in translating this evidence into knowledge products that can travel.

02

Research Focus & Questions

The project addresses the foundational question of how language — as medium of instruction, as marker of identity, as tool of governance — shapes educational opportunity and development outcomes across Africa.

  • RQ1 How does the choice of language of instruction in African schools shape educational access, quality, and equity across different national and linguistic contexts?
  • RQ2 What is the relationship between language policy decisions and measurable social development outcomes — including literacy, school completion, and civic participation?
  • RQ3 How do communities experience and respond to the gap between official language policy and the linguistic realities of their schools and daily lives?
  • RQ4 What do a decade of fieldwork findings suggest about the most effective and equitable language-in-education policy models for diverse African contexts?

Geographic Scope

This is the broadest-ranging INARELS project in terms of geographic footprint, reflecting the accumulated fieldwork of Dr. Odugu and network collaborators across multiple African countries and, notably, in India — which provides a significant comparative frame for understanding the colonial roots of language-in-education policy.

NG

Nigeria

Primary fieldwork site. Nigeria's complex federal language policy — with English as official language and hundreds of indigenous languages — makes it a critical case for understanding the gap between policy and practice.

West AfricaPrimary site
GH

Ghana

Extensive fieldwork on the history of language-in-education policy from the colonial period through present-day curriculum debates about the role of Ghanaian languages in formal schooling.

West AfricaPrimary site
KE

Kenya

Fieldwork covering Kenyan language policy across the independence and post-independence period, with attention to the role of Kiswahili and English as rival markers of national identity in education.

East Africa
TZ

Tanzania

A comparative case where Kiswahili as a national language has produced different educational dynamics than neighboring Anglophone states — providing a valuable contrast point for the broader analysis.

East Africa
ZA

South Africa

Post-apartheid South Africa's 11-official-languages policy offers a complex case study in the politics of language recognition, educational equity, and development outcomes.

Southern Africa
IN

India

Comparative fieldwork at UNESCO headquarters in Paris and in India provides a crucial non-African frame — helping to identify which features of language-in-education challenges are specific to African colonial legacies and which are broadly postcolonial.

Comparative siteSouth Asia

Data Access

Decade-Long Fieldwork Archive

This project holds the largest data archive in the INARELS network — ethnographic notes, interview transcripts, school observation records, and policy documents from eight countries spanning a decade. Access requires INARELS membership and project-specific approval from Dr. Odugu. Certain subsets may require additional data-use agreements.

How to Request Access

  1. 1
    Apply for INARELS Membership

    Submit your bio and research description on the Researchers & Membership page.

  2. 2
    Await Membership Approval

    The INARELS Leadership Team will review your application.

  3. 3
    Request Project-Level Data Access

    Once a member, specify which subset of the Language, Education, and Development archive you need. Dr. Odugu will review.

  4. 4
    Access Data through the Member Portal

    Approved members access data through the project-specific folder in the Member Portal.

Dissemination phase: As the project enters its dissemination phase, a subset of materials may become publicly available through open-access publications. Check the Member Portal for updates or contact Dr. Odugu directly for the latest information.
